Neck pain in the morning: what helps

By the Calm Joints physio team · Updated October 16, 2026 · 10 min read

Waking with a stiff or sore neck is common, and for most people it is a mechanical problem, not a sign that the neck is damaged. A population survey found that 18% of adults reported waking with neck pain and 17% with neck stiffness. Research suggests a supportive pillow, a sleep position that does not twist the neck for hours, and gentle movement after you get up ease morning symptoms more reliably than rest alone.

Why does my neck hurt when I wake up?

Your neck holds the head in a fairly still position for hours. If the pillow is too high, too flat, or stacked so the neck bends sideways, the joints and muscles stay at one end of their range. You feel that as stiffness, a one-sided ache, or pain that spreads toward the shoulder blade.

A telephone survey of 812 adults found waking scapular or arm aching in 27%, headache in 19%, cervical pain in 18%, and cervical stiffness in 17%. Most people said the symptoms lasted more than an hour, on one or two mornings in a week. Women were more likely to wake with headache. Men aged 40 to 59 had a peak in waking neck pain and stiffness. [1]

Morning pain is often worse because you have not moved yet. Joints and muscles that sat still overnight feel guarded. Gentle motion usually eases that within the first hour. Pain that builds through the day, travels down the arm, or comes with weakness is a different pattern, and it is worth a proper look.

Most morning neck pain is what guidelines call non-specific: no single tissue is proven to be the cause, and serious disease is uncommon. The Ontario Protocol for Traffic Injury Management (OPTIMa) guideline groups this as neck pain grades I and II, pain with or without limited movement, without major nerve findings. [2]

Is it the pillow?

Sometimes. A 2021 systematic review and meta-analysis of pillow trials found that rubber pillows (including latex-type fills) reduced neck pain compared with other pillows (standardized mean difference −0.263). Waking pain and neck-related disability also improved, and people were more satisfied with the pillow. Sleep quality itself did not clearly change. The same review found that cervical alignment in side-lying did not reliably differ between rubber and feather pillows. [3]

That is a modest effect, not a cure. A separate pilot trial in people with known neck degeneration did not find one pillow clearly better, and the authors said larger studies are still needed. [4] There is no single best pillow for everyone.

A practical test is simpler than a product claim. On your side, the pillow should fill the gap between ear and mattress so the nose stays roughly in line with the middle of the chest. On your back, the pillow should support the curve of the neck without pushing the chin to the chest. Two pillows stacked usually bend the neck too far. A pillow that has gone flat in the centre often leaves the neck unsupported by morning.

Feather pillows feel soft at first and then collapse. A rubber or spring pillow holds shape longer, which matches the review above. If a new pillow makes the neck worse after a few nights, switch back. Comfort and morning symptoms matter more than the label.

Does sleep position matter?

Side-lying and back-lying are both reasonable if the neck stays near neutral. Stomach sleeping turns the head to one side for most of the night. That is a long hold at end range, and it is a common reason one side of the neck is sore on waking.

Evidence on sleep posture is stronger for the low back than for the neck. A 2025 systematic review linked prone sleeping with more low-back pain and favoured supported side-lying or lying on the back. [5] The neck follows the same logic: a twisted head for six hours is a lot of load on one set of joints.

If you always wake on one side, try a body pillow or a pillow in front of the chest so the top shoulder does not collapse forward. That keeps the neck from side-bending toward the mattress. You will not stay perfectly still. The aim is a better average position, not a rigid pose.

Reading or scrolling in bed with the chin poked forward adds a second load before sleep even starts. A few minutes is fine. An hour with the head tipped down is a common setup for a stiff morning.

What the research says

Ontario's OPTIMa guideline, based on systematic reviews, recommends structured education plus exercise for recent neck pain (about three months or less): range-of-motion exercise, or exercise combined with hands-on care. Education alone, a soft collar, and clinic-based heat were not supported. For neck pain lasting longer than three months, the same guideline supports education plus range-of-motion and strengthening exercise, and also lists yoga, qigong, clinical massage, and multimodal care as options. Strengthening on its own, without education, was not recommended. [2]

A 2015 Cochrane review of exercise for mechanical neck disorders found moderate-quality evidence that neck, shoulder-blade, and arm strengthening can reduce chronic neck pain by a moderate to large amount in the short term. Endurance work for the shoulder blade and arm had a smaller effect. The authors were clear that high-quality evidence was still lacking, and that exercise is a reasonable part of care, not a guarantee. [6]

A 2020 network meta-analysis of 40 trials compared exercise types for chronic non-specific neck pain. Motor-control exercise, strengthening, and yoga, Pilates, tai chi, or qigong all beat no treatment for pain and disability. No single type was clearly superior. The certainty of that evidence was very low, so the practical message is to pick a form you will actually do. [7]

The 2017 neck-pain clinical practice guideline from the Journal of Orthopaedic & Sports Physical Therapy likewise supports exercise, and education that helps people stay active, for neck pain with movement coordination problems. It also stresses screening for conditions that need a different path. [8]

Virtual physiotherapy fits this picture. A 2024 meta-analysis of telerehabilitation for non-specific neck pain found no clear difference in pain versus in-person care, a benefit for disability versus in-person care, and better pain and disability than minimal care. [9] A video visit can cover the education and the exercise program the guidelines already recommend.

In Ontario, government-funded physiotherapy is limited to eligible groups, including people 65 and older, people 19 and under, and some people after a hospital stay or surgery, at designated clinics. [10] Many other adults use extended health benefits. A virtual visit is available across provinces. Calm Joints can talk through what your plan usually covers. Coverage varies by plan.

Should I rest it or move it?

Move it, gently. Collars and full rest are not supported for ordinary neck pain. OPTIMa advised against cervical collars for recent grade I–II neck pain because they were not effective. [2] Staying in one guarded posture tends to keep the morning stiffness around longer.

Heat can feel good for 10 to 15 minutes before you move. That is a comfort step, not a treatment the guidelines rely on. The useful part is the movement that follows.

Pain that stays the same or eases as you walk around the kitchen is reassuring. Pain that spikes with every turn, or that brings arm symptoms, is a reason to scale the exercises back and get assessed.

If mornings are your worst time, a short routine before the shower often works better than a long session later. Ten calm minutes most days beats an occasional hard workout.

Try this today

Stop any of these if pain sharpens, if you feel dizzy, or if tingling or weakness appears in the arm or hand. Mild stretch is fine. A jolt is not.

1. Chin tuck against the wall

Stand with your back to a wall, feet a short step forward. Draw the chin straight back, as if you are making a double chin, without tipping the head up or down. Hold 5 seconds. Repeat 8 times. You should feel a light effort at the front of the neck, not a pinch at the base of the skull. This is the same direction as the motor-control work trials have used for chronic neck pain. [7]

2. Shoulder-blade set

Sit or stand tall. Gently draw the shoulder blades down and slightly together, without shrugging. Hold 5 seconds. Repeat 8 times. Keep the ribs quiet. Cochrane evidence supports strength and endurance work around the shoulder blade as part of care for chronic neck pain. [6]

3. Easy side bend

Sit tall. Tip one ear toward the same shoulder, only as far as a mild stretch along the opposite side of the neck. Hold 15 to 20 seconds. Repeat twice each side. Do not pull with the hand. Skip this if it sends pain into the arm.

4. A one-minute pillow check tonight

Lie on your usual side. Ask someone, or use a phone photo, to see whether your nose lines up with your sternum. If the head is tipped up or dropped toward the mattress, change the fill before you commit to a new pillow. Give any change three or four nights. The pillow review suggests shape-holding fills help some people, and that satisfaction matters. [3]

Physio tip: Check the pillow before you buy another one. On your side, the nose should line up with the middle of the chest. A one-minute photo check often beats a new product claim.

When to see a physio

Book if morning stiffness is still there most days after two weeks, if you are avoiding driving or checking over your shoulder, or if you are losing sleep. A registered physiotherapist can sort recent irritation from a longer pattern, screen for nerve findings, and set a short exercise plan you can do at home.

Seek urgent care, and do not wait for a routine visit, if neck pain comes with any of the following:[11][8]
  • New weakness in an arm or leg, clumsy hands, or unsteady walking.
  • A change in bladder or bowel control.
  • Neck pain after a fall, a hit, or a car collision.
  • Fever, unexplained weight loss, or a history of cancer with new neck pain.
  • Sudden severe neck or head pain with dizziness, double vision, trouble speaking or swallowing, facial numbness, or a drop attack.
Those features can point to nerve or spinal-cord pressure, infection, fracture, or a blood-vessel problem. NICE advises immediate specialist advice when neck-related weakness or sensory loss is severe or getting worse. The 2017 neck-pain guideline also starts with medical screening before exercise is the plan. These need urgent medical care, not a blog and not a booking form. If you are unsure, err on the side of urgent assessment. In an emergency, call 911.

Frequently asked questions

Why is neck pain worse in the morning?

You have held one position for hours, often with the neck slightly bent or twisted. A population survey found waking neck pain in 18% of adults and stiffness in 17%. Movement usually settles the guarded feeling. Pain that worsens through the day needs a closer look.

What pillow is best for neck pain?

Rubber and spring pillows reduced neck pain, waking pain, and disability more than other designs in a 2021 meta-analysis, with a small to moderate effect. No pillow suits every sleeper. Match the height to your position so the neck stays near neutral, and judge it by how you feel after a few nights.

Should I sleep without a pillow if my neck hurts?

Usually no. Too little support lets the neck side-bend on your side or extend on your back. The better test is alignment: ear roughly over the shoulder on your side, chin not forced to the chest on your back.

Do exercises help a stiff neck in the morning?

Yes, for most non-specific neck pain. Ontario's OPTIMa guideline supports education plus range-of-motion exercise for recent pain, and education plus strengthening for longer-lasting pain. Cochrane and a 40-trial review also support strengthening and motor-control work for chronic neck pain, with limits on how certain the evidence is.

Can virtual physio help morning neck pain?

A meta-analysis of telerehabilitation for non-specific neck pain found it comparable to in-person care for pain and helpful for disability, and better than minimal care. A video visit can cover screening, pillow and position advice, and a home exercise plan.

When is morning neck pain an emergency?

Get urgent care if you have new arm or leg weakness, clumsy hands, unsteady walking, bladder or bowel changes, pain after trauma, fever, or sudden neck or head pain with vision, speech, swallowing, or balance changes.
