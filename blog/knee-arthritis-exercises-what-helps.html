Knee Arthritis Exercises: What Helps

By the Calm Joints physio team · Updated November 17, 2026 · 6 min read

Exercise is one of the most effective things you can do for knee osteoarthritis. Research shows it reduces pain and improves how well your knee works, often as well as or better than many other options.[1][2][3] A short virtual visit with a registered physiotherapist can show you which moves fit your knee right now.[5]

Many people with knee arthritis worry that moving will make the joint worse. The opposite is usually true. Stronger muscles around the knee take load off the joint surfaces, and regular movement helps you stay independent.

Why does my knee hurt with arthritis?

Osteoarthritis (OA) is the most common form of arthritis. Cartilage thins, the bone underneath changes, and the joint can feel stiff, swollen or painful with activity. About 15% of Canadian adults over 20 have OA, and more than half of them are under 65.[6]

The pain is real, but it is not always a sign the damage is getting worse. Inactivity often makes things harder: muscles weaken, the joint stiffens, and stairs or getting up from a chair become more difficult.

What the research says

  • The 2019 OARSI guidelines list structured land-based exercise as a core treatment for knee osteoarthritis and recommend it for most people.[2]
  • NICE guidance (2022) puts therapeutic exercise, meaning local muscle strengthening plus aerobic activity, at the centre of care, alongside weight management if needed.[3]
  • A 2024 Cochrane review of 139 trials with more than 12,000 people found that, compared with usual care or no treatment, exercise improved pain by about 13 points on a 0 to 100 scale and physical function by about 12 to 13 points in the short term. Strength, aerobic and mixed programs all helped.[1]
  • The PEAK trial in The Lancet found that video consultations with a physiotherapist were non-inferior to in-person care for chronic knee pain, with similar improvements in pain and function and similar safety.[5]
  • Arthritis Society Canada recommends a mix of aerobic, strengthening and flexibility work, and notes that many people can start with lower-load options such as cycling, water exercise or walking with poles.[4]

Is walking good for knee arthritis?

Yes, for most people. Walking is a low-impact way to build endurance and keep the joint moving. Start with short, comfortable distances and build slowly. If longer walks or stairs cause pain that lasts more than a day, try shorter sessions or a more forgiving surface. See also is walking good for knee arthritis.

Try this today

These are gentle starting points used in many programs. Stop if pain rises sharply or stays higher the next day.

1. Quad set (sitting or lying)

Sit with your leg straight. Tighten the front of your thigh by pressing the back of your knee gently toward the floor. Hold 5 to 10 seconds, relax, and repeat 8 to 10 times. This wakes up the muscle that stabilizes the knee.

2. Straight-leg raise

Lie on your back with one knee bent and the other straight. Tighten the thigh of the straight leg and lift the heel about 15 to 20 cm. Hold briefly, lower slowly, and repeat 8 to 10 times. Stop if the lower back or the opposite hip takes over.

3. Sit-to-stand from a higher chair

Use a sturdy chair. Stand up slowly using your legs as much as possible, then sit back down with control. Do 5 to 8 repetitions. If this is easy, lower the chair slightly over time.

4. Short daily walk

Aim for 10 to 20 minutes at a pace where you can still talk. If one longer walk hurts, split it into two shorter ones. Walking poles or a slight incline can change the load if needed.

Physio tip: Aim for strength work 2 to 3 times a week plus regular aerobic activity most days. Start small and build consistency.

When to see a physio

You do not need to wait until pain is severe. A registered physiotherapist can check your movement, show you which exercises fit your knee right now, and help you progress without flares. Many people find that a few sessions give them a clear plan they can keep using at home. Book a virtual visit at calmjoints.org. Many extended health plans in Canada cover physiotherapy, including virtual visits. For related reading, see knee pain going down stairs and stiff knees in the morning.

Seek same-day medical care if you have:
  • Inability to put any weight on the leg after an injury.
  • A knee that looks deformed or is locked.
  • A hot, red, very swollen knee with fever.
  • Sudden severe swelling right after a twist or fall.
In an emergency, call 911.

Frequently asked questions

Does exercise wear out my knee faster?

No. Guidelines and trials show exercise reduces pain and improves function. Inactivity is more likely to lead to greater difficulty over time. [1][2]

How often should I exercise?

Most programs aim for strength work 2 to 3 times a week plus regular aerobic activity, such as walking, most days. Start small and build consistency. [2][4]

Can I do this if my knee is swollen?

Mild swelling that settles with movement is common. If the knee is hot, very swollen and painful at rest, rest the joint and check with a professional before loading it. [4]

Will I need surgery eventually?

Not necessarily. Many people manage well for years with exercise, weight management if relevant, and activity modification. A physio can help you understand your options. [3]

Does virtual physio work for knee arthritis?

Yes. Trials show video-based physiotherapy produces similar improvements in pain and function to in-person care for chronic knee pain. [5]
