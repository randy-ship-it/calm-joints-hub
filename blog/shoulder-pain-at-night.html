Shoulder pain at night: what helps

By the Calm Joints physio team · Updated October 23, 2026 · 9 min read

Shoulder pain at night is common, and it is often linked to the soft tissues around the joint rather than a single dramatic injury. A sore shoulder can ache more when you lie still on it, and research suggests that shoulder-specific exercise, plus staying as active as you comfortably can, is a sound first step once urgent causes are ruled out.[1][4] A better pillow setup and a short daily strength routine will not fix every shoulder, but they are a practical place to start while you book a proper assessment.

Why does my shoulder hurt more at night?

Daytime movement keeps the shoulder warm and shifts load from one tissue to another. At night that variety stops. If you sleep on the sore side, body weight sits on the joint for hours. A small sleep study of people with subacromial impingement found they spent more sleep time on the painful shoulder than people without that pattern, and they changed position less often.[8] An older clinic study also linked side-sleeping with a higher chance of impingement symptoms.[7] That is an association, not proof that the pillow caused the pain, but it matches what many people notice: the shoulder that was manageable at the desk is the one that wakes them at 2 a.m.

NICE clinical guidance treats night pain and sleep position as part of a normal shoulder history, not as a diagnosis on their own. In primary care, most non-traumatic shoulder pain comes from the tissues around the joint.[6] A 2026 review in JAMA Internal Medicine prefers the plain term subacromial pain over a stack of overlapping labels such as impingement, bursitis, or rotator cuff tendinopathy, because those names often describe the same sore region.[4]

Other reasons night pain shows up:

  • A recent increase in overhead work, a new sport, or a long drive with the arm held out.
  • Stiffness building for weeks, which can be an early frozen shoulder. If motion is shrinking in every direction, our piece on how long frozen shoulder lasts is the closer match.
  • Neck pain that refers into the shoulder. Morning neck ache has its own pattern; see neck pain in the morning.
  • Less often, pain from the joint itself, such as glenohumeral arthritis.

Is night shoulder pain always a torn rotator cuff?

No. Imaging often finds cuff changes in people who sleep fine, and a reported partial tear does not always explain the ache that woke you. NICE notes that ultrasound findings, including partial tears, do not always match symptoms.[6] The JAMA review makes the same point: early imaging is not needed when there is no significant trauma and no suspicious features, because structural findings rarely change the first plan.[4]

What does need a same-day check is a shoulder that suddenly will not lift after a fall or a sharp yank, especially with clear weakness. NICE lists trauma plus pain and weakness, or a sudden loss of the ability to raise the arm, as a reason for urgent referral, to rule out an acute cuff tear.[5]

What the research says

Shoulder-specific exercise is the treatment with the most consistent support.

A 2024 network meta-analysis in PLOS One pooled 54 trials and 3,893 people with chronic shoulder pain. Shoulder-specific exercise reduced pain more than usual medical care, by about 2 points on a 0 to 10 scale (mean difference -2.1, 95% confidence interval -3.5 to -0.7), with follow-up as long as 52 weeks. Adding electrotherapy, injection, or manual therapy on top of that exercise did not clearly add more pain relief. The authors rated the evidence from moderate down to very low, so this is a direction, not a guarantee.[1]

A Cochrane review of manual therapy and exercise for rotator cuff disease found a high-quality trial in which combined treatment beat a placebo ultrasound by a small amount at 22 weeks. The difference in pain was under 7 points on a 100-point scale, which is real but modest. Exercise alone has not shown a clear win over placebo in every comparison, and the review authors were careful not to overclaim.[2]

Surgery is not the default. A 2019 Cochrane review of degenerative rotator cuff tears found that, at one year, repair probably gives little or no important improvement in pain compared with non-operative care (about 0.9 points on a 0 to 10 scale). The trials were mostly small supraspinatus tears, not large traumatic tears.[3] The JAMA review states there is high-certainty evidence that subacromial pain does not benefit from surgery.[4] NICE also says common shoulder problems tend to settle, while warning that rehabilitation can take at least six months.[5]

Sleep position is a contributing factor, not the whole story. The 2012 World Journal of Orthopaedics study found side-sleeping was one of four factors linked with impingement (odds ratio 3.7 versus sleeping on the back).[7] The 2026 sleep-posture study supports the same pattern with measured sleep posture.[8] Neither study proves that changing pillows will cure a cuff problem.

Virtual care can carry this kind of program. An umbrella review of telemedicine for musculoskeletal conditions found patient-reported results that were similar to, or better than, conventional care in most pooled analyses, with lower cost.[9] That fits a plan built on education, load changes, and progressive exercise, which a physio can coach on video.

What helps while you are in bed?

Small setup changes are worth testing for a week before you decide they failed.

  • Stay off the sore shoulder when you can. Back sleeping, or the other side, unloads the joint.
  • If you must lie on the sore side, a pillow in front of the chest that the top arm can rest on stops the shoulder from rolling forward for hours.
  • On the other side, hug a pillow so the top arm does not hang across the body.
  • On your back, a small towel under the upper arm keeps the shoulder from dropping back into the mattress.
  • Keep the room warm enough that you are not hunching. Canadian bedrooms in October and November are a common reason people curl onto one shoulder.

These are comfort experiments. If a new position makes the arm tingle or the pain sharper, drop it.

Try this today

Do these once a day. Mild muscle effort is fine. Sharp pain, pain that lingers more than a couple of hours, or a feeling that the arm may give way means stop and get assessed.

1. Pillow-supported rest position

Lie on your back or on the non-sore side. Place a pillow or folded towel so the sore arm is supported in front of you, elbow slightly bent. Breathe out and let the shoulder drop. Stay for 3 to 5 minutes. Stop if the hand goes numb or the pain climbs.

2. Pendulum hang

Lean on a counter with the other hand, feet shoulder-width. Let the sore arm hang and make small circles, about the size of a plate, for 30 to 60 seconds. Switch direction. Do this once or twice. If the shoulder pinches at the top of the circle, make the circle smaller.

3. Shoulder-blade set

Sit or stand tall, elbows soft. Gently draw the shoulder blades back and slightly down, as if sliding them into your back pockets. Do not shrug. Hold 5 seconds. Repeat 8 times. Stop if the neck starts doing the work or pain spreads into the arm.

4. Isometric outward press

Stand beside a wall with the sore elbow bent to 90 degrees, tucked near your side. A thin towel under the arm can help. Press the back of the wrist gently into the wall without letting the arm move. Hold 5 seconds at an easy effort. Repeat 6 to 8 times. Stop if pain rises above a mild ache, or if you cannot keep the elbow in.

Physio tip: None of these replaces a program matched to your shoulder. They are safe starters a physio can progress.

If desk hours are part of the picture, the same load idea shows up in lower back pain from sitting: change position before the tissue stays irritated all evening.

When to see a physio

Book a visit if night pain has lasted more than a couple of weeks, if you are sleeping in a chair to cope, or if lifting a mug or a coat has started to hurt. A registered physiotherapist can sort subacromial pain from a stiff capsule, check the neck, and set a strength plan you can do at home.

Calm Joints visits are virtual, with registered physiotherapists, for people across Canada. Book a virtual visit at calmjoints.org. Many extended health plans cover physiotherapy, including virtual visits. In Ontario, OHIP does not generally pay for physiotherapy in private clinics for adults, so a benefits check is worth doing before you book.

Seek urgent care, and do not wait for a routine physio slot, if you have:[5]
  • A fall or sudden yank, then clear weakness or you cannot lift the arm.
  • Chest pain, breathlessness, sweating, or pain into the jaw or left arm.
  • Fever, a hot swollen joint, or feeling systemically unwell.
  • Unexplained weight loss, a history of cancer, or night pain that is constant and not tied to position.
  • New numbness, or pain and weakness running down the arm after an injury.
In an emergency, call 911.

A calm next step

Night shoulder pain is unsettling because it steals sleep, and poor sleep makes the next day feel worse. The evidence does not support rushing to a scan or to surgery for a typical sore shoulder. It supports a clear explanation, a kinder sleep setup, and shoulder-specific exercise, reviewed if you are not improving.

Frequently asked questions

Why does my shoulder only hurt when I lie down?

Lying still loads one part of the shoulder for a long time, especially if you are on that side. Daytime movement often hides the same irritation. Position changes and a pillow in front of the chest are reasonable first tests. [7][8]

Can a pillow fix shoulder pain at night?

A pillow can unload the joint and help you sleep, which matters. It will not strengthen a cuff tendon or free a stiff capsule. Use it with a simple exercise plan, not instead of one.

How long does night shoulder pain last?

NICE notes that common shoulder problems often settle, and that rehabilitation can take at least six months. Many people sleep better sooner once load and position change. A plan that is not helping after a few weeks should be reviewed. [5]

Should I get an ultrasound before physio?

Not usually, if there was no real injury and no red flags. NICE and the JAMA review both caution that early imaging often finds changes that do not match the pain and do not change first-line care. [4][6]

Does virtual physio work for shoulder pain?

Research on telemedicine for musculoskeletal pain suggests results similar to in-person care for many patient-reported outcomes, particularly when the treatment is education and exercise. A video visit can assess painful movements, sleep setup, and a home program. Hands-on treatment is not part of a virtual visit. [9]

Is heat or ice better at night?

Either can be a comfort tool. Use a warm pack or a wrapped ice pack for about 10 to 15 minutes, with a cloth between the pack and skin, and stop if the skin is unhappy. Neither replaces exercise or an assessment.
