Tennis questions, answered

What are the most common tennis injuries?

Tennis asks a lot of shoulders, elbows, knees and feet. Common complaints include tennis elbow, shoulder strain from serving, knee pain, and sore feet or ankles from wide, quick steps.

When should I get a tennis injury checked?

If pain keeps coming back, stops you moving normally, or has not settled, talk it through with a registered physiotherapist. Get urgent care for severe pain, a joint that looks out of place, numbness, or if you cannot put weight on a leg. In an emergency, call 911.

Can a physiotherapist assess a tennis injury on video?

Yes. A Calm Joints video assessment is with a registered physiotherapist. They look at how you move and talk through next steps with you, including whether an in-person visit makes sense. Book a time that fits around your matches.

How does the tennis club partnership work?

There are two tiers. Tier 1 (physio only): your club displays a co-branded QR code, anyone who books through it gets sessions with registered therapists across the province, and the club earns a 25% payout on net revenue from those bookings. Tier 2 (full recovery activation): a co-branded member store at wholesale rates, with physiotherapy built in.

Does it take staff time or inventory?

It is designed to be hands-off. The sign, cards and code are made for your club, and booking and care are handled for you. Tier 1 needs no inventory. In Tier 2, orders are handled for you.

Where does the QR code go?

Two options: a 24 by 36 inch stand-up sign at court side, or a compact co-branded sign on the court fence or a nearby wall. The code opens calmjoints.org/tennis.
